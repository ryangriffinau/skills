# Customer mode

Use for a named customer, client or external sponsor. The reader knows their
operation, not the repository. Write every line from the reader's seat: what
they see on their screen, stop doing by hand, stop missing, or can now trust.

## Keep

- Address a named person when the channel and context support it.
- One block, concise. Each item is two sentences at most: what changed for
  them, then any caveat that affects them.
- Keep rollout ownership clear: state readiness, and leave go-live or staff
  exposure decisions with the customer when those decisions are theirs.
- Disclose a degradation the reader could have noticed, with its dates, in
  their terms.
- Use at most one plumbing sentence in the main update. Put valuable technical
  depth in the optional appendix.

## Cut

Delete internal churn: refactors, item totals, ceremonies, backlog clearing,
deployment plumbing, team process (walkthroughs, verification runs,
evaluations, who runs which command) and any sentence whose subject is the
delivery team's effort. Keep operational caveats that affect the customer.

## Shape

1. Direct opener, when useful
2. Actions upfront, when any ask exists
3. The reader's own requests, in their numbering, each with its state.
   Separate section, kept apart from new work, whenever the window answered
   feedback they gave.
4. New capability, ordered by customer visibility
5. Coming next, from the reader's seat: each item names what they will see or
   be able to do, in their vocabulary. A process step that delivers it (a
   walkthrough, a deploy, a data repair, an evaluation) stays in the audit.
   Omit the section rather than fill it with process.

_Mode check:_ every main-section item changes what this reader knows, trusts,
does or decides, and reads correctly if the delivery team is invisible.
